# Change log

## main

### Added

- JetStream: push subscriptions of `js.subscribe` with `idle_heartbeat` check the last consumer sequence that each heartbeat carries (`Nats-Last-Consumer`) against the last message that came, like `js.Subscribe` of nats.go, and report a mismatch, as when messages were dropped for a slow consumer, to the error callback as `NATS::JetStream::Error::ConsumerSequenceMismatch` (`ErrConsumerSequenceMismatch`), with `consumer_sequence`, `last_consumer_sequence` and the `stream_resume_sequence` to resume from.
- JetStream: batched direct gets, like `GetBatch` and `GetLastMsgsFor` of orbit.go jetstreamext, for streams with `allow_direct` (nats-server 2.11): `js.get_batch(stream, batch, seq:, subject:, start_time:, max_bytes:)` gets the messages from a sequence or time on, and `js.get_last_msgs_for(stream, subjects, up_to_seq:, up_to_time:, batch:)` the last message on each subject; both yield the messages as they come until the server ends the batch, or return them, and raise `NATS::JetStream::Error::NoMessages`, `BatchUnsupported` and `InvalidStreamResponse` like `ErrNoMessages`, `ErrBatchUnsupported` and `ErrInvalidResponse`, and `ArgumentError` for invalid options.
- Services: `service.info` and `service.stats` have the `:type` of the monitoring responses, and `service.ping` returns the response to a PING, like `Info`, `Stats` and `Ping` of nats.go micro, whose types are `NATS::Service::PING_RESPONSE_TYPE`, `INFO_RESPONSE_TYPE` and `STATS_RESPONSE_TYPE`; `service.reset` also resets the time the service started, like `Reset`. Services are validated like in nats.go micro: one without a name or a SemVer version raises `NATS::Service::InvalidNameError` or `InvalidVersionError` instead of being created, and service and endpoint names must match as a whole, so that a name such as `calc.v2`, or a name or version with a line break, is refused (group names are checked as before).
- Services: `request.respond_with_error` raises `NATS::Service::ArgRequiredError` for an error without a code or a description, like `ErrArgRequired` of nats.go micro, sending nothing, and `respond`, `respond_json` and `respond_with_error` raise `NATS::Service::RespondError` when the response cannot be sent, like `ErrRespond`, with the error of the client as its `cause`; a handler that rescues it goes on and the endpoint counts the error.
- Services: `endpoints.add` takes `pending_msgs_limit:` and `pending_bytes_limit:`, the pending limits of the endpoint's subscription, like `WithEndpointPendingLimits` of nats.go micro; reaching one stops the service with a slow consumer error, and a limit that is not a positive Integer raises `NATS::Service::InvalidPendingLimitsError`.
- Services: `queue_group_disabled: true` makes the endpoints of a service (`client.services.add`) or group (`groups.add`), or an endpoint (`endpoints.add`), subscribe without a queue group, like `QueueGroupDisabled` and `WithEndpointQueueGroupDisabled` of nats.go micro, unless a `queue:` is set below; `queue_group_disabled?` tells, and such endpoints report a `queue_group` of `""`. A `queue: ""` still disables the queue group.
- Services: the `endpoint:` option of `client.services.add` adds an endpoint named `default` when the service is created, like `Config.Endpoint` of nats.go micro; it takes the options of an endpoint (`subject:`, `metadata:`, `queue:`) and its `handler:`, or the block given to `add`, and a service whose default endpoint cannot be added is not created.
- KV: `kv.purge(key, last: revision)` purges the key only if `revision` is its latest, like `LastRevision` of nats.go, as `kv.delete(key, last:)` already did, raising `NATS::JetStream::Error::WrongLastSequence` otherwise; it can be combined with `ttl:`. `purge` still returns the `PubAck` of the purge marker, whose `seq` is its revision.
- KV: `kv.bucket` names the bucket of a handle, like `Bucket` of nats.go, and `BucketStatus` has `backing_store` (`"JetStream"`), `bytes` and `config`, a `KeyValueConfig` rebuilt from the bucket's stream that `create_key_value` takes again, like `BackingStore`, `Bytes` and `Config` of nats.go `KeyValueBucketStatus`.
- KV: `kv.list_keys` and `kv.list_keys_filtered(*filters)` return a `NATS::KeyLister`, like `ListKeys`, `ListKeysFiltered` and `KeyLister` of nats.go: an Enumerable of the keys, empty for an empty bucket, whose watcher stops once the keys are listed, when the listing is left early, or with `stop` from any thread. `kv.keys` now honors filters (`kv.keys("a.*")`, `kv.keys(["a", "b"])` or `filters:`), yields to a block, and stops its watcher when the iteration is left early instead of leaking it; it still raises `NoKeysFoundError` when there are no keys, like `Keys`.
- KV: `kv.get` returns entries with `created`, the time the bucket stored them, and an Integer `revision`, also with direct gets, like `Created` of nats.go `KeyValueEntry`. Entries of puts have the operation `"PUT"` instead of `nil`; `NATS::KeyValue::Operation::PUT`, `DELETE` and `PURGE` (`"PUT"`, `"DEL"` and `"PURGE"`) are the `KeyValuePut`, `KeyValueDelete` and `KeyValuePurge` of nats.go. `NATS::KeyValue::ALL_KEYS` and `KEY_VALUE_MAX_HISTORY` are `AllKeys` and `KeyValueMaxHistory`, and `RawStreamMsg#time` is when the stream stored a message.
- KV: the bucket and key errors of nats.go, each a subclass of the error raised before so that rescuing that one still works: bucket names that are not of letters, digits, `_` and `-` raise `NATS::KeyValue::InvalidBucketNameError` (an `ArgumentError`) in `create_key_value`, `update_key_value`, `create_or_update_key_value`, `key_value` and `delete_key_value`, like `ErrInvalidBucketName`, and a missing one `BucketRequiredError`; a `nil` config raises `KeyValueConfigRequiredError` (`ErrKeyValueConfigRequired`); creating a bucket that exists with another config raises `BucketExistsError` (`ErrBucketExists`), a `JetStream::Error::StreamNameAlreadyInUse`; and `kv.create` of an existing key raises `KeyExistsError` (`ErrKeyExists`), a `KeyWrongLastSequenceError`.
- KV: buckets can mirror a bucket, or take the keys of other buckets, like `Mirror` and `Sources` of nats.go `KeyValueConfig`: `js.create_key_value(bucket:, mirror: {name:, domain:})` creates a mirror with `mirror_direct`, which reads with gets on the mirror and writes to the origin bucket, through the domain's API prefix when it is in another domain; `sources: [{name:}, ...]` maps the keys of each bucket to the new one with subject transforms (`$KV.<source>.>` to `$KV.<bucket>.>`), unless a source has `subject_transforms` of its own.
- JetStream: `nc.jetstream(client_trace: {request_sent: ->(subject, payload) {}, response_received: ->(subject, payload, header) {}})` traces the requests to the JetStream API and their responses, like `WithClientTrace` of nats.go, including those of stream and consumer management, direct gets, KV and object stores; publishes, pulls and acks are not traced, as in nats.go. A `response_received` that takes two arguments gets no header.
- JetStream: the `mirror` and `sources` of a stream config take a `domain:`, which `add_stream`, `update_stream` and `create_or_update_stream` send as `external: {api: "$JS.<domain>.API"}`, to mirror or source a stream of the JetStream of another domain, like the `Domain` of a `StreamSource` of nats.go, without changing the config given; a source with both raises `ArgumentError`, as nats.go does.
- JetStream: `jsm.account_info` returns a typed `NATS::JetStream::API::AccountInfo`, like `AccountInfo` of nats.go, with `memory`, `storage`, `reserved_memory`, `reserved_storage`, `streams`, `consumers`, `limits` (`AccountLimits`), `domain`, `api` (`APIStats`, with the API `level`) and `tiers` (`Tier`s by name), and takes a `timeout:`. It still reads as the Hash it returned: `[]`, `dig` and `to_h` (which gives that Hash) work, and tier names can be Symbols; code that changed the Hash, as with `delete`, has to call `to_h` first.
- JetStream: like nats.go, the client checks that the server applied the settings that older servers drop. Creating or updating a stream whose `subject_transform` the server's config lacks raises `StreamSubjectTransformNotSupported` (`ErrStreamSubjectTransformNotSupported`), one whose sources it lacks `StreamSourceNotSupported` (`ErrStreamSourceNotSupported`), and one whose sources lack their `subject_transforms` `StreamSourceSubjectTransformNotSupported`, a `StreamSubjectTransformNotSupported`; creating a consumer whose `filter_subjects` the server dropped raises `ConsumerMultipleFilterSubjectsNotSupported` (`ErrConsumerMultipleFilterSubjectsNotSupported`).
- JetStream: stream and consumer handles like the nats.go `jetstream` package. `js.stream(name)` returns a `NATS::JetStream::Stream` (`Stream`) with `info` (taking `subjects_filter:` and `deleted_details:`) and `cached_info`, `purge`, `delete_msg`, `secure_delete_msg`, `get_msg(seq, subject:)`, `get_last_msg_for_subject`, `create_consumer`, `update_consumer`, `create_or_update_consumer`, `consumer(name)`, `delete_consumer`, `consumers`/`list_consumers`, `consumer_names`, `ordered_consumer`, pause, resume, unpin and reset, and the push consumer variants; like nats.go it gets messages directly when the stream allows direct gets. `js.consumer(stream, name)` and those methods return a `NATS::JetStream::Consumer` (`Consumer`) with `info`, `cached_info`, `fetch`, `fetch_bytes`, `fetch_no_wait`, `next`, `consume` and `messages`, through a pull subscription bound to it, raising `NotPullConsumer` for a push consumer; `js.push_consumer(stream, name)` returns a `NATS::JetStream::PushConsumer` (`PushConsumer`) with `info` and `cached_info`, raising `NotPushConsumer` for a pull consumer.
- JetStream: `jsm.stream_info(name, subjects_filter: "orders.>")` returns the number of messages of each matching subject as `state.subjects`, a Hash by subject, like `WithSubjectFilter` of nats.go, requesting every page of 100,000 subjects that the server has, and `deleted_details: true` the sequences of the deleted messages as `state.deleted`, like `WithDeletedDetails`. `StreamState` also decodes `num_deleted`, `num_subjects` and `lost` (a `LostStreamData`), which it dropped.
- JetStream: `jsm.create_or_update_stream(config)` updates a stream, or creates it when it does not exist, in one call, like `CreateOrUpdateStream` of nats.go, returning a `StreamCreateResponse` as `add_stream` and `update_stream` do.
- JetStream: `consume` and `messages` of pull subscriptions and ordered consumers take `stop_after: n`, like `StopAfter` of nats.go, to stop once `n` messages were taken, pulling no more than that (counted across the consumers of an ordered consumer), and `bytes_limit:` to keep `max_messages` asked for with each pull taking at most that many bytes, like `PullMaxMessagesWithBytesLimit`.
- JetStream: push subscriptions of `js.subscribe` take the control messages of their consumer like `js.Subscribe` of nats.go: idle heartbeats are no longer passed to the block or returned by `next_msg`, flow control requests are answered once the messages before them were delivered (and when a heartbeat says the consumer stalled), and with `idle_heartbeat` the error callback gets `NATS::JetStream::Error::ConsumerNotActive`, like `ErrConsumerNotActive`, whenever nothing came for two heartbeats.
- JetStream: the `RawStreamMsg` of `get_msg` and `get_last_msg` has the `time` the message was stored, as a `Time`, like `RawStreamMsg.Time` of nats.go, from the response of a get or the `Nats-Time-Stamp` header of a direct get; a direct get now gives the `seq` as an Integer, as a get does, instead of a String, and so do the revisions of the KV entries read with direct gets.
- JetStream: `opt_start_time` of a consumer config, and of a stream's `mirror` and `sources`, takes a Ruby `Time`, sent as RFC 3339 with nanoseconds like the `time.Time` of nats.go instead of `Time#to_s`. The times that stay RFC 3339 Strings have readers returning a `Time`, or `nil` when unset, like the `time.Time` fields of nats.go: `StreamState#first_time` and `last_time` (`first_ts`, `last_ts`), `SequenceInfo#last_active_time`, and `leader_since_time` and `[:desired].created_time` of the `cluster` of `StreamInfo` and `ConsumerInfo`, now a `ClusterInfo` Hash.
- JetStream: the consumer durations `ack_wait`, `idle_heartbeat`, `inactive_threshold` and `priority_timeout` take fractions of a second, as Floats such as `0.5`, sent as exact nanoseconds like the `time.Duration` fields of nats.go `ConsumerConfig`, as does the `inactive_threshold` of `js.ordered_consumer`; a fetched config keeps Integers for whole seconds and has a Float only when the server's value has a fraction, instead of rounding it down. `backoff` and `max_expires` stay in nanoseconds.
- Subscriptions: `sub.valid?` tells whether a subscription is still active, like `IsValid` of nats.go: not once it was unsubscribed, got its max messages or was drained, or its connection was closed. `sub.pending` returns the messages and bytes that wait for the callback or `next_msg`, like `Pending`, and `sub.queued_msgs` the messages, like `QueuedMsgs`; both raise `NATS::IO::BadSubscription` for a subscription that is not valid.
- Errors of messages and subscriptions like nats.go, as subclasses of those raised before: a received message whose header cannot be decoded comes without it and `on_error` gets `NATS::IO::BadHeaderMsg` (`ErrBadHeaderMsg`), where the header was parsed as it could; once its messages were taken, `sub.next_msg` raises `MaxMessages` (a `BadSubscription`, `ErrMaxMessages`) for a subscription that got its max messages, `BadSubscription` for one that was unsubscribed or drained and `ConnectionClosedError` once the connection is closed, at once and also when waiting, instead of a `NATS::Timeout` at the end of the timeout; `msg.respond` and `respond_msg` raise `MsgNoReply` (a `BadSubject`, `ErrMsgNoReply`) for a message without a reply and `MsgNotBound` (`ErrMsgNotBound`) for one that did not come from a connection, which they ignored; and `publish_msg`, `request_msg` and `respond_msg` raise `InvalidMsg` (a `TypeError`, `ErrInvalidMsg`) for what is not a `NATS::Msg`.
- Subscriptions: `sub.set_pending_limits(msgs, bytes)` changes the pending limits of a subscription once subscribed, like `SetPendingLimits` of nats.go, and `sub.pending_limits` returns them, like `PendingLimits`. A negative limit does not limit, also as the `pending_msgs_limit:` and `pending_bytes_limit:` of `subscribe`, and zero raises `ArgumentError` (`ErrInvalidArg`); a closed subscription raises `NATS::IO::BadSubscription`. Writing `sub.pending_msgs_limit` also resizes the queue now: raising it beyond the limit given to `subscribe` blocked the reading of the connection once that many messages were pending.
- `NATS.new_inbox` returns a unique inbox subject without a connection, like `NewInbox` of nats.go, and `NATS.new_inbox(prefix)` one with the prefix, checked as the `custom_inbox_prefix` option is; `nc.new_inbox` still uses that of its connection. An empty `custom_inbox_prefix` now raises `NATS::IO::ClientError`, like nats.go, instead of giving inboxes that start with a dot.
- Messages: `msg == other` (and `eql?`/`hash`) compares the subject, reply, header and data of two messages, not their connection or subscription, like `Msg.Equal` of nats.go, and `msg.size` gives its size in bytes as the server counts it against `max_bytes` (subject, reply, header and data), like `Msg.Size`; that of a received message is its size as it came.
- `no_callbacks_after_client_close: true` keeps `nc.close` from calling `on_disconnect` and `on_close`, like `NoCallbacksAfterClientClose` of nats.go; they are still called when the client closes the connection itself, as when a drain completes or it gives up reconnecting, and subscriptions are still told with their `on_close`.
- `nc.on_reconnect_error { |e| }` is called with the error of each attempt to reconnect that fails, like `ReconnectErrCB` of nats.go, and with that of the first connect and of its retries with `retry_on_failed_connect`; the errors still go to `on_error` too, and `reconnect_error_handler` returns the callback.
- `retry_on_failed_connect: true` makes `connect` return at once, reconnecting, when it cannot connect to any server, like `RetryOnFailedConnect` of nats.go: it keeps trying in the background as `max_reconnect_attempts` and `reconnect_time_wait` allow, buffering what is published and subscribed meanwhile, calls `on_connect` once connected and closes the connection, calling `on_close`, once out of attempts. By default `connect` still blocks while it retries.
- `ignore_auth_error_abort: true` keeps reconnecting to a server that refused the credentials with an auth error, like `IgnoreAuthErrorAbort` of nats.go, for credentials from a `token_handler` or the like that may change: by default the client stops reconnecting to a server once it closed the connection with an auth error (an authorization violation, or expired or revoked credentials) and refused it once more, which closes a connection with a single server, while with the option it retries as `max_reconnect_attempts` allows.
- `write_buffer_size:` (bytes) makes a publish that leaves that many bytes pending write them out itself, waiting for the socket, like `WriteBufferSize` of nats.go, instead of leaving them all to the flusher thread; and `flusher_timeout:` (seconds) bounds how long a write may block, like `FlusherTimeout`: a write that the socket does not take in time raises `NATS::IO::SocketTimeoutError` to `on_error` and the client reconnects. By default, as before, nothing forces a flush and writes may block for good (nats.go uses 32 KiB and a minute).
- `skip_host_lookup: true` connects to the host name of the server as it is, like `SkipHostLookup` of nats.go, leaving it to the system to resolve it, and hands `custom_dialer` the host name; by default the client resolves the name itself and dials its addresses in turn, in the order of the resolver (nats.go also shuffles them).
- `custom_dialer:` connects with a dialer of your own, like `SetCustomDialer` of nats.go: an object that responds to `dial(host, port, timeout)`, or a Proc called with them, that returns a connected socket, called for each address the host name of the server resolves to (or the host name itself if it does not resolve) until one connects. A dialer whose `skip_tls_handshake?` is true returns connections that are secure already, and the client does no TLS handshake of its own, like `SkipTLSHandshake` of nats.go.
- WebSocket: `proxy_path:` is the path of the upgrade request, like `ProxyPath` of nats.go, for servers behind an HTTP proxy that routes by path: it takes the place of the path of the `ws://` or `wss://` URL, keeping its query, and gets a leading `/` when it has none.
- WebSocket: `ws_headers:` (a Hash; an Array value sends the header once per value) adds HTTP headers to the upgrade request, and `ws_headers_handler:`, a Proc called on every connect and reconnect, returns them instead, like `WebSocketConnectionHeaders` and `WebSocketConnectionHeadersHandler` of nats.go, for example to pass credentials in a cookie that the server takes them from. Setting both raises `ArgumentError` like `ErrWebSocketHeadersAlreadySet`, what the handler raises fails the connect attempt, and headers that would break the request raise `NATS::IO::WebSocket::HandshakeError`.
- WebSocket: `compression: true` asks the server to compress the messages both ways with permessage-deflate, like `Compression` of nats.go, without context takeover; against a server that does not compress, the connection goes on uncompressed. The client now does the WebSocket upgrade and framing itself, so the `websocket` gem is no longer needed, and it answers the pings of the server, takes fragmented messages and fails on a close frame instead of parsing it as data; messages over `ws://` and `wss://` are binary Strings, as over `nats://`.
- Subscriptions: `sub.drain` drains a subscription, like `Subscription.Drain` of nats.go: it unsubscribes and closes the subscription in the background once the messages received until the server confirmed it were processed, raising `NATS::IO::BadSubscription`, `ConnectionClosedError` or `ConnectionDrainingError` as nats.go does, and `sub.draining?` tells whether it is under way, like `IsDraining`. `sub.dropped` counts the messages dropped as a slow consumer, like `Dropped`, `sub.max_pending` returns the most messages and bytes that were pending, like `MaxPending`, and `sub.clear_max_pending` resets them. `sub.on_close { |subject| }` is called once a subscription with a callback is closed, like `SetClosedHandler`: after it was unsubscribed or got its max messages and processed the messages it got, once its drain is done, or when the connection closes.
- `nc.barrier { ... }` runs the block once the messages that the subscriptions with a callback received so far have been processed, like `Barrier` of nats.go, also when a subscription processes them concurrently (`processing_concurrency`); without such subscriptions it runs right away. A closed connection raises `NATS::IO::ConnectionClosedError`.
- Connection introspection like nats.go: `nc.connected_addr` and `nc.local_addr` (`ConnectedAddr`, `LocalAddr`) give the addresses of the connection, `nc.connected_url_redacted` (`ConnectedUrlRedacted`) the connected URL with its password or token replaced by `xxxxx`, `nc.num_subscriptions` (`NumSubscriptions`) the number of subscriptions and `nc.buffered` (`Buffered`) the bytes waiting to be sent. What the server tells is read with `connected_server_id`, `connected_server_name`, `connected_server_version`, `connected_cluster_name`, `client_id`, `client_ip`, `max_payload`, `headers_supported?`, `auth_required?`, `tls_required?` and `jetstream?`, and the callbacks set with the `on_*` methods with `error_handler`, `disconnect_handler`, `reconnect_handler`, `close_handler`, `connect_handler`, `discovered_servers_handler` and `lame_duck_mode_handler`.
- `nc.set_server_pool(urls)` replaces the server pool, like `SetServerPool` of nats.go, without reconnecting: the next reconnect uses the new pool, and a server that stays in it keeps its state, the current one going last. Invalid URLs, and websocket URLs mixed with others (`ErrMixingWebsocketSchemes`), raise `ArgumentError` and leave the pool as it was; a closed connection raises `NATS::IO::ConnectionClosedError`.
- `nc.rtt` measures the round trip time to the server, in seconds, like `RTT` of nats.go. It raises `NATS::IO::ConnectionClosedError` once the connection is closed and `NATS::IO::Disconnected`, a new `ClientError` like `ErrDisconnected`, while it is not connected, as while reconnecting.
- JetStream: `js.publish_async(subject, data)` publishes without waiting for the ack, like `PublishAsync` of nats.go, taking the options of `js.publish`, and returns a `NATS::JetStream::PubAckFuture`, whose `ack`, `err` and `msg` are those of `Ok`, `Err` and `Msg`, and whose `wait(timeout)` returns the ack or raises the error. The acks come to a single subscription of the context, with a token per message. `js.publish_async_pending` and `js.publish_async_complete(timeout:)` are those of `PublishAsyncPending` and `PublishAsyncComplete`. Options of `nc.jetstream`: `publish_async_max_pending` (4000) beyond which `publish_async` stalls for `publish_async_stall_wait` (0.2 seconds, or `stall_wait:` per call) and then raises `TooManyStalledMsgs`, like `PublishAsyncMaxPending` and `WithStallWait`; `publish_async_timeout` (or `timeout:` per call), after which a future fails with `AsyncPublishTimeout`, like `PublishAsyncTimeout`; and `publish_async_err_handler`, called with the message and the error of each failed publish, like `PublishAsyncErrHandler`. A message that no stream responds to is retried as with `js.publish`, then fails with `NoStreamResponse`.
- JetStream: `js.publish` retries a message that no stream responded to, as when a stream is electing a leader, like nats.go: twice, 250ms apart, by default (`DefaultPubRetryAttempts` and `DefaultPubRetryWait`). `retry_attempts:` (negative for as long as the timeout allows) and `retry_wait:` (seconds) change that per publish, or for the context as options of `nc.jetstream`. A publish to a subject that no stream takes now raises `NoStreamResponse` after the retries, some 0.5 seconds later than before, unless its timeout ends first, as the retries share the timeout; `retry_attempts: 0` raises it at once, as before.
- JetStream: the JetStream API errors that nats.go names raise classes of their own, each a subclass of the error raised before, such as `BadRequest`, so that rescuing that one still rescues them: `StreamNameAlreadyInUse`, `ConsumerNameAlreadyInUse`, `MaximumConsumersLimit`, `DuplicateFilterSubjects`, `OverlappingFilterSubjects`, `EmptyFilter`, `WrongLastSequence`, the message-schedule errors such as `MessageSchedulesDisabled` and `ScheduleTargetInvalid`, `MsgNotFound` (`NotFound`), `ConsumerCreate` (`ServerError`), and `JetStreamNotEnabled` and `JetStreamNotEnabledForAccount` (`ServiceUnavailable`). Fetches ended by a 409 status raise `ConsumerDeleted`, `ConsumerLeadershipChanged` or `ServerShutdown`, all `API::Error`s, like `ErrConsumerDeleted`, `ErrConsumerLeadershipChanged` and `ErrServerShutdown` of nats.go. Like nats.go, the client raises `NotPullConsumer` and `NotPushConsumer` when a pull or push subscription binds to the other kind of consumer, `MsgNotBound` and `MsgNoReply` when acking a message that no subscription delivered or that has no reply, `InvalidJetStreamResponse` for an API response that is not a JSON object, and `InvalidJSAck` for a publish response that is not an ack, instead of `JSON::ParserError`, `NoMethodError` and the like.
- JetStream: `jsm.stream_names` and `jsm.streams` list the names and the `StreamInfo` of the streams, like `StreamNames` and `Streams` of nats.go, optionally only those that take a `subject:` (wildcards allowed), like `WithStreamListSubject`; `jsm.consumer_names(stream)` and `jsm.consumers(stream)` list the names and the `ConsumerInfo` of the consumers of a stream, like `ConsumerNames` and `Consumers`. They return Arrays, requesting as many pages as the server has.
- JetStream: `js.ordered_consumer(stream, filter_subjects:, deliver_policy:, opt_start_seq:, opt_start_time:, replay_policy:, inactive_threshold:, headers_only:, max_reset_attempts:)` reads a stream in order, like `OrderedConsumer` of the nats.go `jetstream` package: from an ephemeral pull consumer that does not ack, keeps its state in memory and has one replica, which it creates again from the next stream sequence it expects when it misses a message, the heartbeats stop, the connection reconnects or the consumer is deleted. It reads with `fetch` and `next`, or with `consume` and `messages`, as pull subscriptions do, and reports the current consumer with `consumer_info`. Mixing the two, or reading twice at once, raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::OrderedConsumerUsedAsFetch`, `OrderedConsumerUsedAsConsume` or `OrderedConsumerConcurrentRequests`, like the `ErrOrdered*` errors of nats.go.
- JetStream: pull subscriptions consume continuously, like `Consume` and `Messages` of the nats.go `jetstream` package: `psub.consume(max_messages:, max_bytes:, expires:, heartbeat:, threshold_messages:, threshold_bytes:, error_handler:) { |msg| ... }` passes each message to the block in a thread of its own, and returns a `ConsumeContext` with `stop`, `drain`, `closed?` and `wait_closed`; `psub.messages(...)` returns a `MessagesContext` iterator with `next(timeout:)`, `each`, `stop`, `drain` and `closed?`. Both keep up to `max_messages` (500 by default) or `max_bytes` asked for, pull again below the threshold, when the idle heartbeats stop and after a reconnect, and stop when the consumer is deleted. They raise or report `NATS::JetStream::Error::NoHeartbeat`, `ConsumerDeleted` and `MsgIteratorClosed`, like `ErrNoHeartbeat`, `ErrConsumerDeleted` and `ErrMsgIteratorClosed`; `fetch` now raises `ConsumerDeleted`, an `APIError`, for a deleted consumer.
- JetStream: `fetch(batch, heartbeat: seconds)` asks the server for idle heartbeats while the pull waits, like `PullHeartbeat` of nats.go, and ends when it hears nothing for two of them, as when the server is gone: it returns what it got, or, with no messages, raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::NoHeartbeat`, like `ErrNoHeartbeat`. The heartbeat must be less than half the timeout, and cannot be used with `no_wait`.
- JetStream: `fetch(batch, max_bytes: n)` takes at most `n` bytes, as the server counts them against the pull (subject, reply, header and data), like `FetchBytes` and `PullMaxBytes` of nats.go. The fetch ends once it has the batch or the bytes, or as soon as the next message would exceed them; when that is its first message, it raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::MaxBytesExceeded`, like the `ErrMaxBytesExceeded` that nats.go returns for a fetch with no messages.
- Object Store, wire compatible with nats.go `jetstream` and the other clients: `js.create_object_store`, `update_object_store`, `create_or_update_object_store`, `object_store`, `delete_object_store`, `object_store_names` and `object_stores` manage stores (`ObjectStoreManager`), and `NATS::ObjectStore` has `put` (a String or an IO, with `ObjectMeta`), `put_string`, `put_file`, `get` (returning the data, or writing it to an `io:`, checked against its SHA-256 digest), `get_bytes`, `get_string`, `get_file`, `get_info`, `update_meta`, `delete`, `add_link`, `add_bucket_link`, `seal`, `watch`, `list` and `status`, raising the errors of nats.go as `NATS::ObjectStore::ObjectNotFoundError`, `DigestMismatchError`, `LinkNotAllowedError` and so on.
- KV: `watch` and `watchall` take `updates_only: true`, to get only the updates made after the watch starts, without the `nil` update, and `resume_from_revision:`, to get the entries from a revision on, like `UpdatesOnly` and `ResumeFromRevision` of nats.go. A watcher whose consumer is recreated keeps to them.
- KV: `kv.purge_deletes` removes the data of deleted and purged keys, like `PurgeDeletes` of nats.go, and their markers once older than `older_than:` seconds, like `DeleteMarkersOlderThan`: 30 minutes by default, and all markers when negative.
- KV: per-key TTLs and limit markers (nats-server 2.11). Buckets created with `limit_marker_ttl:` (seconds) keep the markers that the server leaves when a TTL removes a key, as `status.limit_marker_ttl` reports, like `LimitMarkerTTL` of nats.go. `kv.create(key, value, ttl:)` and `kv.purge(key, ttl:)` give the key or its purge marker a TTL, like `KeyTTL` and `PurgeTTL`; `kv.delete` with a `ttl:` raises `NATS::KeyValue::TTLOnDeleteNotSupportedError`. `get` and `watch` take the server's markers (`Nats-Marker-Reason`) for deletes and purges.
- KV: `js.update_key_value(config)` and `js.create_or_update_key_value(config)` change the configuration of a bucket, or create it, like `UpdateKeyValue` and `CreateOrUpdateKeyValue` of nats.go; updating a bucket that does not exist raises `NATS::KeyValue::BucketNotFoundError`. `js.key_value_store_names` and `js.key_value_stores` list the names and the `BucketStatus` of the buckets, like `KeyValueStoreNames` and `KeyValueStores`.
- JetStream: fast-ingest batch publishing to streams with `allow_batched` (nats-server 2.14), like the `FastPublisher` of orbit.go jetstreamext and on the same wire: `js.new_fast_publisher` publishes with `add` and `add_msg`, which return a `FastPubAck` and wait only while more than `max_outstanding_acks:` acks of `flow:` messages are outstanding, pinging the server for lost acks, and ends the batch with `commit`, `commit_msg` or `close`, returning a `BatchAck`. An `error_handler:` receives the gaps the server reports, as `NATS::JetStream::Error::FastBatchGapDetected`, which end the batch unless `continue_on_gap: true`, and the messages it refuses. The server's fast batch errors are named as in orbit.go, such as `FastBatchNotEnabled`, with the error codes of nats-server.
- JetStream: atomic batch publishing, like the `BatchPublisher` and `PublishMsgBatch` of orbit.go jetstreamext: `js.new_batch_publisher` publishes messages with `add` and `add_msg`, which take a `ttl:`, the expected `stream:`, a `msg_id:` and expected last sequences, and commits them with `commit`, `commit_msg` or `close` (an end-of-batch marker, nats-server 2.14), returning a `BatchAck`, or abandons them with `discard`; `js.publish_msg_batch(msgs)` publishes an Array in one call. Both wait for acks on the first message and every `ack_every:` messages. The client raises `BatchClosed`, `EmptyBatch` and `InvalidBatchAck`, and the server's batch publish errors are `BadRequest` errors named as in orbit.go, such as `BatchPublishIncomplete`, with the error codes of nats-server. Streams need `allow_atomic` (nats-server 2.12).
- Services: `NATS::Service.control_subject(verb, name = nil, id = nil)` builds the `$SRV` subject to ping, or get the info or stats of, services, like `ControlSubject` of nats.go micro, raising `NATS::Service::VerbNotSupportedError` or `ServiceNameRequiredError` like `ErrVerbNotSupported` and `ErrServiceNameRequired`. `NATS::Service::ERROR_HEADER` and `ERROR_CODE_HEADER` name the error headers, like `ErrorHeader` and `ErrorCodeHeader`.
- Services: `request.respond`, `respond_json` and `respond_with_error` take `headers:` for the response, like `WithHeaders` of nats.go micro; on an error response they are added to the error headers and can override them.
- Services: `request.respond_json(obj)` responds with `obj` as JSON, like `RespondJSON` of nats.go micro. An object that cannot be generated as JSON raises `NATS::Service::MarshalResponseError` (`ErrMarshalResponse`), and nothing is sent.
- Services: an error handler, set with the `error_handler:` option or `service.on_error`, receives the service and a `NATS::Service::NATSError` (`subject`, `description`) when one of the service's subscriptions fails, like a slow consumer or a NATS error raised in a handler, before the service stops, like `Config.ErrorHandler` and `NATSError` of nats.go micro. Services also stop when their connection is closed, as in nats.go.
- Specific errors like nats.go's, as subclasses of the generic ones: the `-ERR`s of the server are `NATS::IO::AuthorizationViolation`, `AuthenticationExpired`, `AuthenticationRevoked` and `AccountAuthenticationExpired` (`AuthError`s), and `PermissionViolation`, `MaxSubscriptionsExceeded`, `MaxConnectionsExceeded` and `MaxAccountConnectionsExceeded` (`ServerError`s). On the client side, `publish` raises `MaxPayload` for a message larger than the server's `max_payload` and `HeadersNotSupported` for headers the server does not support, `subscribe` raises `BadSubject` for a subject with whitespace and `BadQueueName` for such a queue name, `flush` raises `BadTimeout` (a `Timeout`) for a timeout that is not positive, and `drain` while reconnecting closes the connection and raises `ConnectionReconnecting`, like nats.go (all `ClientError`s, except `BadSubject`); `NoInfoReceived`, `SecureConnRequired` and `SecureConnWanted` are `ConnectError`s.
- Connection callbacks like nats.go: `on_connect` is called once a connection is first established, before `connect` returns (`ConnectedCB`; reconnects call `on_reconnect`), `on_discovered_servers` when the server announces servers that join the pool (`DiscoveredServersCB`), and `on_lame_duck_mode` when the server enters lame duck mode (`LameDuckModeHandler`). What they raise goes to `on_error`.
- Auth callbacks and in-memory credentials like nats.go: `token_handler:` (`TokenHandler`) and `user_info_handler:` (`UserInfoHandler`) are Procs called on every connect for the token and for `[user, password]`; `user_credentials_data:` takes the contents of a credentials file (`UserCredentialBytes`), and `user_jwt:` with `user_seed:` a JWT and its seed (`UserJWTAndSeed`). Options that conflict raise `ArgumentError`, like `ErrTokenAlreadySet`, `ErrUserInfoAlreadySet`, `ErrNkeyAndUser`, `ErrUserButNoSigCB` and `ErrNkeyButNoSigCB` of nats.go; that includes `user_credentials` with `nkeys_seed`, which used `nkeys_seed` only, and `user_jwt_cb` or `user_nkey_cb` without `user_signature_cb`, which were ignored.
- TLS: `tls: {cert_file:, key_file:}` presents a client certificate and `tls: {ca_file:}` trusts only the CAs of that file, like `ClientCert` and `RootCAs` of nats.go, without building an `OpenSSL::SSL::SSLContext`; combining them with `context:` raises `ArgumentError`. `tls_handshake_first: true` does the TLS handshake before reading the server's INFO, like `TLSHandshakeFirst`, for servers with `handshake_first` in their `tls` block. `tls://` and `wss://` URLs in `servers:` now get a default TLS context, as a URL String did; they failed with a `TypeError`.
- `reconnect_buf_size:` bounds the bytes of publishes buffered while reconnecting, 8 MiB by default, like `ReconnectBufSize` of nats.go; a negative size disables the buffering. A publish once the buffer is full raises `NATS::IO::ReconnectBufExceeded`, a `ClientError`, instead of buffering more, or blocking until reconnected once 32768 commands were buffered.
- Reconnect tuning like nats.go: `reconnect_jitter:` and `reconnect_jitter_tls:` (`ReconnectJitter`) add a random delay of up to that many seconds, by default 0.1 and 1 (for TLS), to each wait of `reconnect_time_wait` before reconnecting to a server, and `custom_reconnect_delay:` (`CustomReconnectDelay`), a Proc given the attempts made so far, returns the seconds to wait instead.
- `connect_timeout` also bounds the TCP dial and the TLS handshake, like `Timeout` of nats.go; the dial always took up to 2 seconds (`DEFAULT_CONNECT_TIMEOUT`) and the handshake had no limit. Both raise `NATS::IO::SocketTimeoutError` when it is up.
- `no_echo: true` stops the server from delivering a connection's own publishes to its subscriptions, like `NoEcho` of nats.go. A server that does not support it (protocol 0) makes connect raise `NATS::IO::NoEchoNotSupported`, a `ConnectError`.
- JetStream: `jsm.delete_msg(stream, seq)` and `jsm.secure_delete_msg(stream, seq)` delete a message from a stream, like `DeleteMsg` and `SecureDeleteMsg` of nats.go; the secure variant overwrites the message's data. A deletion that the server does not confirm raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::MsgDeleteUnsuccessful`.
- JetStream: `jsm.purge_stream(name)` purges a stream, like `PurgeStream` of nats.go: all its messages, or only those on a `subject:` (wildcards allowed), those below a sequence (`seq:`), or all but the latest `keep:` ones. It returns a `StreamPurgeResponse` with the number of messages `purged`. `seq:` and `keep:` together raise `ArgumentError`.

### Fixed

- `msg.respond(data)` on a message with headers published a copy of the message, so the response carried the headers and the reply subject of the request. Like `Respond` of nats.go, it now publishes only the data to the reply subject. `msg.respond_msg(response)` now publishes the response to the reply subject of the message, which becomes its subject, like `RespondMsg`; it was published to its own subject.
- JetStream: the futures of `js.publish_async` that await their acks did not end when the connection was lost or closed, so that `wait` without a timeout and `publish_async_complete` waited for good. Like nats.go, they now fail with `NATS::IO::Disconnected` (`ErrDisconnected`) once the connection is lost, and with `NATS::IO::ConnectionClosedError` once it is closed, calling `publish_async_err_handler`, without replacing the `on_disconnect` and `on_close` callbacks of the connection. After a close, the next `publish_async` subscribes to the acks again.
- Headers: a header name that a message has more than once kept only its last value. Like the `http.Header` based headers of nats.go, received headers now give such a name an Array of its values, while names that come once keep a String; `publish`, `publish_msg`, `request` and `request_msg` take Array values, sending a line for each. This holds for core and JetStream messages, and for the headers of `jsm.get_msg`, also with `direct: true`.
- `nc.connected?` was `false` while the connection drained. Like `IsConnected` of nats.go, it is now `true` until the drain closes the connection, as the connection still publishes and delivers messages; `draining?` tells the drain apart. As in nats.go, a connection that fails while draining still closes instead of reconnecting, and sends no keepalive PINGs.
- `sub.next_msg` on a subscription with a callback raised `NoMethodError`. It now raises `NATS::IO::SyncSubRequired`, a `ClientError`, like `ErrSyncSubRequired` of nats.go.
- KV: `create` and `update` raised the server's `APIError` instead of `KeyWrongLastSequenceError` for the conflicts of replicated (R>1) buckets, which the server reports with err_code 10164 rather than 10071, so `create` could not recreate a deleted key. Both codes are now taken for a wrong last revision, like nats.go.
- KV: `create_key_value` dropped the `placement` of the bucket, so its stream was created without it. It now passes it on to the stream config, like `CreateKeyValue` of nats.go.
- Services: `request.respond` and the `$SRV` monitoring responses no longer echo the headers and the reply subject of the request, like `Respond` of nats.go micro. A monitoring request without a reply subject is ignored, instead of failing the service.
- A permissions violation, or a subscription refused as there are too many, made the client disconnect and reconnect, failing what was in flight, such as a `flush`, and on a server that requires auth it was an `AuthError` that also marked the server as one not to reconnect to. Like nats.go, the connection now stays up, and `on_error` gets a `NATS::IO::PermissionViolation` or `MaxSubscriptionsExceeded`, which are `ServerError`s. Connections refused as the server or account has too many are `ServerError`s too, and no longer mark the server.

## v2.7.0

This release adds the JetStream features of nats-server 2.10 to 2.14 that the
client lacked, such as per-message TTLs, message schedules, and consumers that
can be paused, reset and serve priority groups. `fetch` now pulls once and
waits, like `Fetch` of the nats.go `jetstream` package, and a subscription read
with `next_msg` no longer drops every message after its first 64 MiB. Code that
passed stream or KV settings the client used to drop now sends them; see Changed.

### Added

- JetStream: `fetch(batch, no_wait: true)` takes what the server delivers at once, without waiting for more messages, like `FetchNoWait` of nats.go and `fetch` of nats.rs: an empty Array when none are pending, or when other pulls wait for more than are pending. While the consumer may not deliver more until some are acked (`max_ack_pending`), the server holds the pull back, and the fetch returns what it got once its timeout is up, 1 second by default. (#195)
- JetStream: `fetch` takes a block, to which it passes each delivery as it comes, in the calling thread, so that the message can be acked before the `ack_wait` of the consumer is up: `psub.fetch(100, timeout: 60) { |msg| msg.ack }`. It still returns the messages. (#195)
- JetStream: stream configs take the settings added in nats-server 2.10: `compression`, `first_seq`, `subject_transform` and `consumer_limits` (whose `inactive_threshold` is in nanoseconds, like the other stream durations). They used to be dropped, so a stream could not be created with them, and sending a fetched config back as an update left them out. (#191)
- JetStream: stream configs take the settings added in nats-server 2.11 to 2.14: `allow_msg_ttl` and `subject_delete_marker_ttl` (2.11; the latter in nanoseconds, like the other stream durations), `allow_msg_counter`, `allow_atomic`, `allow_msg_schedules` and `persist_mode` (2.12), and `allow_batched` (2.14). Like nats.go, the client does not send them at their defaults, such as `false`, as servers since 2.12, and 2.11 ones in strict mode, refuse settings they do not know. (#194)
- JetStream: `NATS::JetStream::Header` names the JetStream headers: those for deduplication and optimistic concurrency, such as `MSG_ID` and `EXPECTED_LAST_SUBJECT_SEQUENCE`, and those of nats-server 2.11 to 2.14: `EXPECTED_LAST_SUBJECT_SEQUENCE_SUBJECT`, `MSG_TTL` and `MARKER_REASON` (2.11), the atomic batch headers (2.12; `BATCH_COMMIT` `"eob"` needs 2.14), the message schedule headers (2.12 and 2.14), and `REQUIRED_API_LEVEL` (2.12), which JetStream API requests send as a `header:` option, as in `js.stream_info(name, header: {...})`. It also names the headers that the server sets on the messages it republishes, sources or returns from direct gets, such as `STREAM` and `SEQUENCE`. (#194)
- JetStream: `js.publish` takes `ttl:`, the whole seconds after which the stream removes the message, or `:never` to keep it past the stream's `max_age`, for streams with `allow_msg_ttl` (nats-server 2.11). Other values raise `ArgumentError`. (#194)
- JetStream: `js.publish` takes `schedule:`, which makes the message a schedule that publishes it to its `target:` subject: once, `at:` a Time (nats-server 2.12), `every:` so many seconds, or on a `cron:` expression with seconds, in UTC or a `time_zone:` (2.14). The schedule can give the messages it publishes a `ttl:` (2.12), and publish the last message of a `source:` subject instead and roll up the target (`rollup: true`) (2.14; 2.12 ignores both). The stream needs `allow_msg_schedules`. An invalid schedule raises `ArgumentError`, before anything is sent; `NATS::JetStream::Header::SCHEDULE_NEXT` and `SCHEDULER` cancel a schedule. (#194)
- JetStream: `stream_info` reports the stream's `mirror` and `sources`, including their subject transforms, and both `stream_info` and `consumer_info` report when the server reported the info, as `ts`. (#191)
- JetStream: `create_consumer` and `update_consumer` use the create and update actions of nats-server 2.10. Creating a consumer that exists with a different config raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::ConsumerAlreadyExists`, and updating one that does not exist raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::ConsumerDoesNotExist`; both are `BadRequest` errors. An update replaces the whole config, so fields left out take their defaults. `add_consumer` still creates or updates. (#191)
- JetStream: `msg.term(reason: "...")` passes the reason on to the server's advisory for the terminated message. Servers before 2.10.4 do not terminate a message given a reason. (#191)
- JetStream: consumers can be paused (nats-server 2.11): created paused with `pause_until` in their config, as a `Time` or an RFC 3339 String, or paused and resumed with `pause_consumer` and `resume_consumer`; updating a consumer keeps its pause. `consumer_info` reports whether a consumer is `paused`, and the seconds until it resumes, as `pause_remaining`. Servers before 2.11 create consumers unpaused, and let `pause_consumer` and `resume_consumer` time out. (#193)
- JetStream: pull consumers can serve priority groups (nats-server 2.11). Consumer configs take `priority_policy`, `priority_groups` and `priority_timeout` (in seconds, like the other consumer durations), and `consumer_info` reports the state of the groups as `priority_groups`. `fetch` takes the `group` to pull from, `min_pending` and `min_ack_pending` for the `"overflow"` policy, and `priority` for the `"prioritized"` policy of nats-server 2.12. (#193)
- JetStream: with the `"pinned_client"` priority policy, a pull subscription keeps the pin id that the server delivers with its messages, and sends it with its pulls. When the server says that the subscription is no longer pinned, as its pin expired or it was unpinned with the new `unpin_consumer`, a fetch that got no messages raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::PinIdMismatch`, and the next fetch can be pinned again. The server counts the consumer's `priority_timeout` from each pull, not while a pull waits, so keep the fetch timeout, plus the time between fetches, below it, or the subscription loses its pin. (#193, #195)
- JetStream: `reset_consumer` makes a consumer deliver again from after its ack floor, or from the stream sequence given as `seq` (nats-server 2.14). A sequence that the deliver policy of the consumer does not let it be reset to raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::ConsumerInvalidReset`, a `BadRequest` error, and one that is not an integer of 0 or more raises `ArgumentError`. For a stream or consumer that does not exist, and with servers before 2.14, the request times out. (#193)
- JetStream: `stream_info` and `consumer_info` report the `cluster` of the stream or consumer as a Hash, including when its leader was elected, as `leader_since` (nats-server 2.12). `consumer_info` used to drop it. (#193)
- KV: buckets can be created with `compression: true` and with `metadata`, which `status.compressed?` and `status.metadata` report. (#191)
- KV: `watch` takes an Array of keys, watched by a single consumer (nats-server 2.10). An empty Array watches all keys. (#191)

### Changed

- JetStream: `fetch` pulls once, for the whole batch, and waits: it returns as soon as it has the batch, and otherwise the messages that came when its timeout is up, like `Fetch` of the nats.go `jetstream` package and `batch` of nats.rs. A fetch of more than one message used to ask first, without waiting, for the messages that were pending. When it got some, it then waited out its timeout without pulling for more, so it neither took the messages that came meanwhile nor returned sooner. When other pulls of the consumer waited for more messages than were pending, the server turned that first pull away (408 Requests Pending), and `fetch` raised `NATS::IO::Timeout` at once. (#195)
- JetStream: a pull subscription subscribes to `<inbox>.*`, which `subject` now returns, and each pull gets a reply subject of its own under it, as ADR-13 describes, so that a fetch tells the status that ended its own pull from those that ended the pulls of other fetches. A `fetch` with `no_wait: true` pulls with a subscription of its own, which it drops when done, so that its pull does not linger when the server holds it, as while the consumer may not deliver more messages until some are acked (`max_ack_pending`): the fetch then returns what it got once its timeout is up, and leaves what comes later to the next fetch. A server with leafnodes or gateways delivers to a pull until it is 2 seconds old even once the subscription is gone, so the subscription stays until then. (#195)
- JetStream: `fetch` raises `ArgumentError` for a `timeout` that is not a finite positive number, such as 0, with which it used to raise `NATS::IO::Timeout` at once. It leaves the options it is given unchanged; it used to set their `:timeout`. (#195)
- JetStream: as `fetch` always pulls with an expiry of its timeout, a consumer whose `max_expires` is shorter than the timeout turns every fetch away, with 409 "Exceeded MaxRequestExpires", raised as `NATS::JetStream::API::Error`. A fetch of more than one message used to get the messages that were pending first, with a pull that had no expiry. Pass a `timeout` no longer than the consumer's `max_expires`. (#195)
- JetStream: `fetch` returns when the server ends its pull, so it takes its timeout plus the round trip to the server. When no end comes, it waits up to 1 second longer, like nats.go, before it returns what it got or raises `NATS::IO::Timeout`. (#195)
- JetStream and KV: settings that used to be dropped are now sent. Code that already passed `compression`, `first_seq`, `subject_transform` or `consumer_limits` to `add_stream`, or `compression` or `metadata` to `create_key_value`, created its streams and buckets without them, so calling it again for an existing stream or bucket now fails with "stream name already in use with a different configuration" (err_code 10058). Apply the setting with `update_stream`, or stop passing it. (#191)
- JetStream: the same goes for the settings of nats-server 2.11 to 2.14: code that passed them to `add_stream` created its streams without them, so calling it again for an existing stream now fails with err_code 10058. `update_stream` applies them, except `allow_msg_counter` and `persist_mode`, which a stream cannot change. (#194)
- JetStream: consumer configs now send `pause_until`, `priority_policy`, `priority_groups` and `priority_timeout`, which used to be dropped. As `add_consumer` creates or updates, code that already passed the priority settings to it now gives an existing consumer priority groups, after which the server turns away pulls that name no group (400 "Priority Group missing"). (#193)
- KV: `create_key_value` takes only `true` or `false` for `compression`; anything else, such as `"s2"`, which used to be ignored, raises `ArgumentError`. (#191)
- JetStream: `add_consumer` leaves the `ConsumerConfig` it is given unchanged. It used to set its `name` and a default `ack_policy`, and convert its durations to nanoseconds, so passing the same config again sent durations a billion times too long. (#191)
- JetStream: two `stream_info` or `consumer_info` results are no longer `==` unless they were reported at the same time, as they now carry `ts`, and the `cluster`, `mirror` and `sources`, whose lag and activity change all the time, even with servers that do not report `ts`. (#191, #193)
- KV: `watch` no longer looks up the bucket's stream before creating its consumer. (#191)

### Fixed

- JetStream: `update_stream` with the config from `stream_info` failed with err_code 10052, such as "message TTL status can not be disabled", for streams with per-message TTLs, subject delete markers, counters, message schedules or async persistence, and turned off atomic and fast batches, as `StreamConfig` dropped the settings of nats-server 2.11 to 2.14. It now keeps them. (#194)
- JetStream: `StreamConfig` dropped `discard_new_per_subject`, so `add_stream` created streams without it, and `update_stream` with the config from `stream_info` turned it off: a subject that held `max_msgs_per_subject` messages then discarded its oldest message instead of refusing the new one. It now keeps it. (#199)
- JetStream: `js.publish` with `stream:` added `Nats-Expected-Stream` to the header Hash it was given, so a caller that reused the Hash sent it with its later publishes. (#194)
- JetStream: `js.publish` raised `ArgumentError: unknown keywords` after the server had stored the message, when publishing to a stream with counters or committing an atomic batch, as the acks of nats-server 2.12 carry `val`, `batch` and `count`. `PubAck` now has these fields and ignores any it does not know, so fields added to acks later cannot break publish. (#192, #177, thanks @jnowakplacewise)
- JetStream: `add_consumer`, `consumer_info`, `subscribe` and `pull_subscribe` raised `NoMethodError` for consumers that do not ack (`ack_policy: "none"`) created without an `ack_wait`, after the consumer had been created. The server omits `ack_wait` for them, and `config.ack_wait` is now `nil`. (#192)
- JetStream: `fetch` took a status that an earlier pull had left in the subscription, such as the 408 of a fetch that timed out, for the reply to its own pull, and raised `NATS::IO::Timeout` at once, even with messages pending: a fetch of more than one message with any such status, a fetch of one message with a second one. `fetch` now first takes what earlier pulls left, and pulls for the rest only. And a fetch that got messages before an error status, such as 409 Consumer Deleted, raised the error and dropped them; like nats.go, it now returns them. (#193, #195)
- JetStream: when threads fetched from the same subscription at once, a fetch could take the 408 that ended the pull of another for the end of its own, and raise `NATS::IO::Timeout` before its timeout. (#195)
- JetStream: `fetch` pulled with an expiry a little short of its timeout only after a first pull, which did not wait, came back empty, so its pull outlived it by the time that first pull took: a message delivered meanwhile waited in the subscription for the next fetch, and, if none came, was delivered again once the `ack_wait` was up. A fetch now waits for the server to end its pull, which expires with its timeout. (#195)
- JetStream: a `fetch` that waited longer than the `ack_wait` of the consumer returned a message twice when the server delivered it again meanwhile. It now returns each message once, as last delivered. (#195)
- JetStream: `fetch` took a message of the stream with a `Status` header for a status of the server, and raised an error or ended without it. Statuses are now only the messages with a `Status` header that the server sends without a reply. (#195)
- JetStream: a `fetch` of one message raised `TypeError: nil can't be coerced into Float` when threads fetched from the same subscription at once: woken up for a message that another thread took, it did not wait on. It now waits until its timeout. (#193, #181, thanks @route)
- JetStream: a `fetch` of one message raised `NATS::IO::Timeout` ("nats: fetch request timeout") before its timeout when a status that ended an earlier pull, such as the 408 of a fetch that had just timed out, came while it waited, and left its own message for the next fetch. Like nats.go, it now waits on for its message until its timeout. (#193)
- JetStream: `fetch` could miss the reply to its pull when it came before the fetch waited for it, as when its thread was preempted: a fetch of one message then waited out its timeout and raised `NATS::IO::Timeout`, leaving its message to be redelivered after the ack wait, and a fetch of more messages returned only the first one, after its timeout. `fetch` now takes what is there before it waits. (#193)
- KV: after its consumer was recreated, as after a server restart, a watcher yielded the entries of every key in the bucket instead of the watched keys. The recreated consumer now keeps the keys filter. (#192)

## v2.6.0

This release prepares nats-pure for nats-server 2.16, whose new JetStream
acknowledgement format breaks JetStream consumers and KV watchers in earlier
versions, and fixes a deadlock that could hang `Client#close`.

### Changed

- With `reconnect: false`, losing the connection now closes the client, as nats.go does: the status becomes `CLOSED`, the close callback is called, and `publish` raises `NATS::IO::ConnectionClosedError`. It used to stay `DISCONNECTED` and keep accepting publishes that were never sent, because the close, started from the read loop, killed the read loop part way through. (#189)

### Fixed

- JetStream: accept the 11-token acknowledgement subjects that nats-server 2.16 sends by default (the v2 format of ADR-15). Earlier versions fail to read the metadata of every JetStream message from such a server, which breaks pull and push consumers and KV watchers. (#185)
- JetStream: an unrecognized acknowledgement subject raises `NATS::JetStream::Error::NotJSMessage` instead of `NameError`, and subjects whose numeric fields do not parse are rejected. (#185)
- JetStream: message metadata timestamps keep their full nanosecond precision. (#185)
- KV: watchers track their position from message metadata, so they resume after a server restart with either acknowledgement format. (#185)
- `Client#close` could hang forever. It stopped the client's background threads with `Thread#exit`, and killing a thread that is waiting for the client lock can lose Ruby's mutex wakeup, leaving `close` asleep on an unlocked lock. The threads are now asked to stop and joined, on close and on reconnect. (#183, #189)
- A close while a reconnect was under way did not stick: the reconnect carried on, connected again and restarted the client, subscriptions included. (#189)
- A subscription with a concurrency limit could run its callbacks in parallel, and so out of order: an empty pending queue released the concurrency permit twice. (#186)
- A subscription could stop processing messages for good after a reconnect: shutting down the executor during dispatch killed the read loop and leaked the concurrency permit. (#182)
- WebSocket: protocol lines sharing a frame, such as the `PONG` and `INFO` that nats-server 2.12.2 sends together, were misparsed (#174, thanks @artyomb), and bytes that arrived with the reply to the connect handshake were lost. (#182, #186)
- JRuby: a refused connection raises `Errno::ECONNREFUSED`, as on MRI. (#182, #186)

### Improved

- Tested against nats-server 2.14, 2.15 and the upcoming 2.16 (`main`), on Ruby 3.0 to 3.4 and JRuby. (#182, #188)
